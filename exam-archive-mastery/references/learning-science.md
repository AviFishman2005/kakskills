# Learning science behind the path

Read this before proposing a learning path. It is what the plan is justified by. Re-check the sources if the user
asks for more than the summary, and say where the evidence is thin.

| Finding | Source | What it means for the design |
|---|---|---|
| Practice testing and distributed practice got the highest utility rating; rereading, summarizing and highlighting got a low one. | Dunlosky et al. 2013, *Psychological Science in the Public Interest* ([APS summary](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/releases/which-study-strategies-make-the-grade.html)) | Every rung is a question to answer, not text to read. |
| Successive relearning: practice to a criterion, then return in spaced sessions and reach it again. In courses, exam scores were more than 10% higher. One recall in each of three spaced sessions beat three recalls in one session, 68% vs 26% at one week. More than about three relearning sessions added little. | Rawson & Dunlosky 2022, *Current Directions* ([link](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/09637214221100484)) | Three correct in a row to learn a skill, then reviews at about +1, +3 and +7 days, compressed to the exam date, each needing one correct answer. |
| Faded worked examples: removing solution steps from the end first improved near transfer; far transfer was mixed. | Renkl, Atkinson, Maier & Staley 2002 ([summary](https://www.structural-learning.com/post/worked-examples)) | The ladder: worked example → completion with the last steps hidden → fewer steps shown → alone. |
| Expertise reversal: examples help novices and stop helping, or hurt, as knowledge grows. | Kalyuga, Chandler, Tuovinen & Sweller 2001; meta-analysis Tetzlaff et al. 2025 | An optional diagnostic lets a student who already knows a skill skip to solving alone. |
| Interleaving problem types beat blocking in classroom RCTs, because the student must choose the method. Textbooks rarely interleave (9.7% of problems). | Rohrer, Dedrick & Hartwig 2020 ([link](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s10648-020-09516-2)) | Mixed rounds, plus "which pattern is this?" drills on real exam wording. |
| Parsons problems (ordering lines) raised practice efficiency, but the post-test difference was not significant. | Hou, Ericson & Wang 2023, ITiCSE ([link](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10510493)) | Use line ordering only as optional help, never as the main exercise. |
| Pretesting helps when feedback follows, but the evidence is mostly about facts, not multi-step procedures. | Richland, Kornell & Kao 2009 ([summary](https://www.structural-learning.com/post/pretesting-effect-testing-before-teaching)) | The diagnostic is for placement, not sold as a learning booster. |
| Self-explanation prompts showed smaller worked-example benefits in one meta-analysis, which doesn't show they harm. | Barbieri et al. 2023 | Keep a single "why" question per example, not a prompt at every step. |

Additions that fit an exam specifically:

- **Readiness weighted by points**: compute average points per topic from the archive, and weight mastery by
  them.
- **Real grader deductions as distractors and feedback**: they are the most exam-specific signal there is.
- **Calibration**: the student rates confidence before checking. "Sure but wrong" items are what to revisit
  first. Keep the claim modest; don't promise a hypercorrection effect.
- **Answer-direction statistics** (e.g. 55% "not true" in ER statements) are context, not a guessing strategy.
  Say so on the page.
